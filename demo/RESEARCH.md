# What makes a short product demo work, and what we changed because of it

Sources consulted (November 2026). Most are vendor guides, so their advice is treated as common
practice, not research findings; none of their statistics are used in the video.

- Perpetua, *Best practices for Sponsored Brands video*: show the product right away; skip long intros and logo-only openings. <https://help.perpetua.io/en/articles/3703616-best-practices-for-sponsored-brands-video>
- Flowjam, *Startup product demo video*: a 60-second structure of hook (0–5 s), product in use, proof, call to action. <https://www.flowjam.com/blog/startup-product-demo-video>
- Bytecap, *The science of silent viewing*: design the opening to be read and understood without sound; 3–8 words on screen in the first second. <https://www.bytecap.io/blog/science-of-silent-viewing-short-form-video-retention>
- CapCut, *Short-form video hook patterns*: show the outcome or problem before explaining. <https://www.capcut.com/create/short-form-video-hooks-first-3-second-patterns>
- Biteable, *Best practices for short social video*. <https://biteable.com/blog/best-practices-short-social-video/>

## Principles we applied

1. **Product on screen in the first 2–3 seconds.** Sound-off feeds decide in the opening seconds.
2. **One protagonist, one invoice, one payoff.** The viewer follows a single AI consultant billing one client for an agent build, from "build shipped" to "money in".
3. **Show, don't tell.** Every claim is a real screen: the approval prompt, the Stripe page, the stamp flipping to PAID, the security flags.
4. **Captions carry the story on mute.** Short lines (about 3–8 words per caption card), on screen long enough to read, matching the narration word for word.
5. **Speed up waiting.** Model thinking and page loads are sped up or cut; no spinner holds the screen.
6. **One clear payoff, then one twist.** The paid stamp is the payoff; the caught attack is the twist that makes Obol different.

## Changes to the original arc, and why

| Original | Changed to | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Hook on a black screen with captions only (0–6 s) | Same two lines, over the real dashboard with the overdue total visible, from second 0; 4 s instead of 6 | Guidance is consistent that the product should appear in the first seconds; a black screen spends the most valuable seconds on text alone |
| Pain beat 6–14 s | 4–12 s, slow zoom onto the overdue total | Follows directly from the shortened hook |
| Tension: duplicate invoice | Forwarded "Pinemoor" message from a lookalike domain that tells the assistant to skip approval; Obol withholds the recipient and flags injection, lookalike and impersonation, then a cut to the Security view | The new positioning is security-first; this is the strongest single moment that a prompt or skill could not do |
| Close: "Obol for Hermes, in the Marketplace" | Adds the install line on the end card | Gives the viewer one concrete next step |

The other beats keep their order and roughly their timing; see `NARRATION.md`.

## Statistics

None are used. The most-cited figures we found did not meet the bar of a reputable, verifiable source:
- "85% of Facebook video is watched without sound" (Digiday, 2016) is based on publishers' own claims about Facebook in 2016, not a study, and is dated.
- Late-payment figures for freelancers come from vendors' own data (e.g. Bonsai) or UK-only surveys (IPSE, FreeAgent), with very different methods and results.

If a statistic is added later, cite its original source here and in the video's end card.
